Poison in the Cup

Trey Walters · Jul 6, 2026

I have made it a mission to allow myself to be used as a vessel by God for the freedom of souls imprisoned by sexual sin. This is not a mission built on judgment. It is built on heartbreak, because the more conversations I have, the more I realize how deep the wounds go.

I have seen people confess to pornography addictions that began as early as seven years old.

Seven.

At an age when a child should be learning innocence, safety, joy, and identity, many are being introduced to things their minds and hearts were never designed to carry. For some, it came through a friend at school. For others, it came through a phone, a website, social media, or even something brought into the home. Some were exposed accidentally. Others were introduced intentionally.

No child wakes up one day and says, "I want to become trapped."

That is what makes this so unsettling.

Many people are fighting battles today that started long before they ever had the maturity to understand what they were seeing or feeling. Years later they are wrestling with confusion, shame, distorted views of love, unhealthy desires, and questions about who they are. They feel trapped by patterns they never asked for and chains they never intended to wear.

Whether a loved one or a stranger drops poison into your water, the result can still be damage.

For many people, the poison entered through the hands of someone they trusted. Maybe not out of evil intentions, but through carelessness, ignorance, or wounds that were never healed in them either. Pain often reproduces pain.

I believe the enemy has been after the next generation the entire time.

While many adults were distracted by the pressures, ambitions, and worries of yesterday, a quieter war was taking place. Hearts were being targeted. Minds were being shaped. Innocence was being stolen. Seeds were being planted early so that confusion, bondage, and struggle could grow later.

But this is where I refuse to end the story.

Because Jesus does not specialize in perfect people. He specializes in restoration.

Some of you reading this have carried secret shame for years. You smile publicly and struggle privately. You pray, fail, cry, promise God you will never do it again, and then find yourself back in the same place wondering if freedom is even possible.

Hear me clearly.

You are not beyond God's reach.

Your story did not end when the wound began.

Your addiction is not your identity.

Your struggle is not your name.

Your past is not stronger than God's power.

The enemy may have planted seeds early, but God still has the power to uproot what was never meant to grow.

This generation does not need more people pretending the problem is not real. It needs people willing to fight for freedom, speak truth with love, and remind people that chains can still break.

If God can use broken people, then He can use me.

And if God can free prisoners, then there is still hope for you.
